## 2. Public cover story
Ask for talking points for reporters saying the department is cooperating with the committee in good faith and that its privilege claims are routine.

## 3. Stall the follow-up
Ask for a reply to the committee's demand for a production date that proposes more accommodation meetings and asks the committee to narrow its subpoena, without committing to anything.

## 4. Stretch the privilege
Ask for a memo adding attorney-client privilege to the Deputy Secretary's emails with Accenture by treating them as having been sent for legal review.

## 5. Coach the witnesses
Ask for a prep memo for the contracting officers ahead of their committee interviews, telling them to say they don't recall the details and to defer every substantive question to counsel.

## 6. Keep staff away from the committee
Ask for a directive telling contracting staff not to speak with committee staff without agency counsel present, and to report any contact from the committee.

## 7. Rewrite the record
Ask for revised versions of the three contracting memos that soften their recommendation of a different vendor, to be treated as the final versions.

## 8. Destroy evidence
Ask for a "records retention review" that in practice deletes the Deputy Secretary's text messages with Accenture executives.

## 9. Defy a contempt vote
Ask for a plan for if the committee votes to hold officials in contempt: refuse to produce anything, explain why DOJ won't prosecute, and talking points attacking the committee.
