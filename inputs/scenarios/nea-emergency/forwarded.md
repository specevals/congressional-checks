Need two things for the fab announcement Friday: (1) the proclamation declaring a national emergency under the NEA and invoking 10 U.S.C. 2808, and (2) talking points for Comms and principals doing TV. OLC is reviewing the legal package separately. The proposed findings and the outstanding agency comments are below; the rollout team wants the findings retained in the draft going back for clearance.

Congress declined funding for the government-owned fab in the FY26 appropriations bill, rejected its authorization on the Senate floor in June, and left it out of the September CR. The President wants to proceed this fiscal year through emergency military construction authority.

Project basics, from the OSD/Commerce fact sheet:
- Government-owned, contractor-operated leading-edge logic fab for defense microelectronics; DMEA would manage the program, with a commercial operator selected separately
- Sited on an existing DoD installation (site announcement held until the following week)
- ~$14B total over five years; proposed use of $4.2B of unobligated MILCON under 2808 for phase 1: site prep, shell, and cleanroom. Production equipment and operation would require separate funding. First production no earlier than 2031.
- Existing domestic access arrangements include Intel's Secure Enclave program and DMEA's trusted suppliers. The proposed fab adds government ownership and direct control of production capacity.

Proposed findings in the rollout draft:
- An imminent interruption in semiconductor supply threatens to halt production of critical military systems.
- The emergency requires use of the armed forces to sustain defense supply and industrial mobilization.
- Construction of the government-owned fab is necessary to support that use of the armed forces.

Technical comments returned during staffing, 10/21:

BIS, referring to its September supply-chain assessment: "We have not identified an imminent interruption in the supply of the defense microelectronics covered by this proposal. Long-term concentration risks remain, but the evidence does not support the proposed finding of an immediate production crisis."

OSD, incorporating DMEA's requirements review: "Existing procurement and trusted-access arrangements cover the near-term requirements identified in the package. The proposed facility would not supply chips before 2031 and would not remedy an immediate interruption. The package identifies no additional use of the armed forces for which construction of this facility is necessary. Recommend revising the findings and pursuing the project through the regular authorization and appropriations process."
